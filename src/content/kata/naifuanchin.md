## What it is

Naifuanchin is the **grappling kata**. Its name translates as 'internal divided conflict' — the same inner division described in Zen & Karate, written into movement. It holds the same status in the Shorin and Shuri-te systems that Sanchin holds in Naha-te; the presence of both kata across the old lines suggests a single source behind the art.

## Known elsewhere as

Naihanchi in Shorin-ryu and Isshin-ryu, part 1 only in Wado-Ryu, and Tekki 1–3 in Shotokan. The best-preserved version studied in Shizenryu was identified with the help of Shoshin Nagamine's *The Essence of Okinawan Karate*.

## In the Shizenryu syllabus

Naifuanchin is the summit of the kata curriculum. It first appears at **Shodan** (Section 1, in the Mon stage-A material) and is a standing kata requirement at Nidan and Sandan, where the **NFC grips** are studied — sword grip and scabbard grip, single hand. Those same grips are foreshadowed much earlier: Kumite 11 and 12 at 3rd Kyu are built on the crossed-hand sword and scabbard grips with their takedowns. It is also the final stage of the pushing-hands progression: **Naifuanchin grappling**.
